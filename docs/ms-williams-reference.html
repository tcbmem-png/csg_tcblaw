The rebuttable presumption of § 43-19-101 may be overcome by a written finding under § 43-19-103 that application of the guideline percentage would be unjust or inappropriate, based on the ten enumerated criteria. Each factor below shows: the statute verbatim, both parties' positions in their own counsel's words, supporting facts and documentation, proposed dollar adjustments, and the chancellor's decision surface. a
Extraordinary medical, psychological, educational, or dental expenses
Both assert — different amounts
§ 43-19-103(a): Extraordinary medical, psychological, educational or dental expenses.
Per counsel for Obligor (Robert Williams) −$1,800/mo
Obligor pays $1,800/month directly to private school for both children. The children have attended St. Mary's School since 2021 and continued enrollment is consistent with their pre-separation lifestyle. This direct payment should reduce the guideline support amount because the presumptive percentage cannot reasonably include extraordinary educational expenses of this magnitude on top of all other obligations.
Supporting facts
Tuition and fees $1,800/mo × 12 = $21,600/year, paid by Obligor since 2021. Both children enrolled, on track to continue through 12th grade.
Documentation

Legal authority
§ 43-19-103(a); Tedford v. Dempsey, 437 So. 2d 410 (Miss. 1983).

The net deviation should reflect Obligor's direct payment minus Obligee's pro rata share of expenses she carries.

g
Particular shared parental arrangement (50/50 custody)
Both assert — different amounts
§ 43-19-103(g): The particular shared parental arrangement, such as where the noncustodial parent spends a great deal of time with the children thereby reducing the financial expenditures incurred by the custodial parent, or the refusal of the noncustodial parent to become involved in the activities of the child, or giving due consideration to the custodial parent's homemaking services.
Per counsel for Obligor −$1,200/mo
Obligor exercises true 50/50 physical custody — 182.5 days/year with each child. Obligor maintains a separate household sized for two children, with bedrooms, food, clothing, transportation, and activities for the children's time in his home. The presumptive 20% percentage was calibrated against typical noncustodial-visitation arrangements (a few weekends and holidays); applying it without adjustment to 50/50 produces a result that double-counts what Obligor is already paying directly. A meaningful downward adjustment is required to reflect the actual division of household expense.
Supporting facts
50/50 schedule in effect since September 2024; Obligor's household carries housing, food, clothing, transportation, and entertainment expenses for the children half of every month.
Documentation
Parenting plan dated 2024-09-15 (Exh. R-1); household budget showing children's expenses in Obligor's home (Exh. R-9).
Legal authority
§ 43-19-103(g). Mississippi case law gives chancellors discretion on the magnitude of the (g) adjustment in 50/50 cases.
Per counsel for Obligee −$600/mo
Obligee concedes that 50/50 parenting warrants some downward adjustment but disputes the magnitude. Obligee's household provides the children's primary residence stability — school enrollment, medical providers, social context, extracurricular logistics — all of which generate fixed costs that do not halve simply because the children are physically with the other parent half the time. A 25% downward adjustment ($1,150/mo reduction from presumptive) overstates the actual cost shift; a smaller adjustment of $600/mo more accurately reflects the marginal cost savings to Obligee.
Supporting facts
Obligee maintains a five-bedroom home in school district necessary for the children's attendance, with utilities, mortgage, and operations costs that are not reduced by 50/50.
Documentation
Obligee's household budget (Exh. S-9).

§ 43-19-103(g).

Total available assets of obligee, obligor, and the child
Both assert — different amounts
§ 43-19-103(h): Total available assets of the obligee, obligor and the child.
Per counsel for Obligor −$300/mo
Obligee inherited approximately $400,000 from her mother in 2024. The inheritance is held in a brokerage account that has generated investment income averaging $1,500/month over the past 18 months. This represents a substantial increase in resources available to support the children beyond Obligee's school counselor salary. A modest downward adjustment of $300/month reflects the resources Obligee can deploy from the inheritance independently of the support order.
Supporting facts
2024 estate distribution of $400,000; brokerage statements showing average $1,500/month income.
Documentation
Estate inventory (Exh. R-12); brokerage statements 2024–2026 (Exh. R-13).
Legal authority
§ 43-19-103(h).
Per counsel for Obligee $0
Obligee disputes that her inheritance is properly considered under factor (h) for purposes of reducing Obligor's support obligation. The inheritance is separate property under Mississippi law; Obligor's far greater income and assets remain the controlling consideration. Factor (h) considers "total available assets" of all parties, not just Obligee's. Obligor's net worth is significantly larger and any (h) analysis weighing both sides' assets counsels against reduction.
Supporting facts

j
Equitable result — catchall
Obligor only
§ 43-19-103(j): Any other adjustment which is needed to achieve an equitable result which may include, but not be limited to, a reasonable and necessary existing expense or debt.
Per counsel for Obligor −$500/mo
Beyond the specific factors above, Obligor seeks a further $500/month downward adjustment under (j) to achieve an equitable result. Obligor's combined direct contributions to the children's lifestyle — private school, healthcare premiums, activities, the carried cost of 50/50 housing and food — already substantially exceed what the presumptive amount would provide. The catchall is available to prevent a result where the support order layered on top of these direct contributions produces total provision well in excess of the children's reasonable needs.
Legal authority
§ 43-19-103(j) — equitable result catchall.
Per counsel for Obligee $0
Obligee disputes the (j) catchall as duplicative of the (a) and (g) deviations already granted in Obligor's favor. The catchall is intended for circumstances not captured by the enumerated factors; here, Obligor's circumstances are fully captured by (a), (g), and (h). Layering an additional (j) deduction risks reducing support below what is required to maintain the children's pre-separation standard of living in Obligee's household.
